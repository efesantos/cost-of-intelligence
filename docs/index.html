Cost of Intelligence: affordability explorer

How many minutes of local work does it take to buy the same amount of AI output? Pick a language, a country and a tokenizer family. The figures recompute from measured tokenizer fertility, OpenRouter list prices and three wage bases. This is an affordability measure only: tokens sell at one US-dollar price everywhere, so it says nothing about currency values.

Why two World Bank figures? Tokens are priced in US dollars and paid for at market exchange rates, so the market-rate figure is the like-for-like one. The PPP figure shows the burden as if tokens were priced like local goods. It is lower in every country here.

Bundle size. Minutes scale exactly with the bundle, so moving the slider never changes the order of countries. One million English-equivalent tokens is a yardstick: about 750,000 words of English, by the common rule of thumb of three-quarters of a word per token.

Adoption. Countries where the bundle costs more work use Claude less per head, but in the paper's 23-country analysis this link disappears once national income is taken into account. It is an association, not a cause.

Caveats that apply to every figure
  • One global price: affordability only, no claim about exchange rates or currency values.
  • The output-to-input ratio r is an assumption (default 3).
  • Fertility belongs to a tokenizer, not a language; always read it with the family.
  • Prices are API list prices, not subscription plans.
  • Payment access and service availability are not measured.
  • Fertility is measured on formal written text (FLORES-200), not chat.
  • Output tokens are assumed to have the same fertility as input text.
