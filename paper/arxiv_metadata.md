## Abstract (1759 of 1,920 characters)

```
Large language models are sold at one US-dollar price per token, but a token is not a fixed unit of meaning: many languages need more tokens than English to say the same thing. We extend "The African Language Tax" (Somide, 2026) from cost to affordability and adoption, and from African languages to the Lusophone and Hispanophone Global South. We measure tokenizer fertility for 28 languages and six tokenizer families on FLORES-200, combine it with OpenRouter list prices retrieved on 2026-10-10 and three wage bases (World Bank GNI per capita at market exchange rates and at purchasing-power parity, and ILOSTAT mean earnings of employees), and define four family-stratified instruments. The main one counts the minutes of local work needed to buy one million English-equivalent tokens a month. At the gpt-4o price, that bundle costs about 11 minutes of work in the United States. In Amharic in Ethiopia it costs about 1,620 minutes at PPP wages and about 5,280 minutes at market-rate wages, roughly 140 and 460 times as much. Wages, not fertility, drive most of the spread across countries. We then relate the index to the Anthropic Economic Index for 23 countries. The rank correlation with adoption is strong (Spearman rho = -0.85), but affordability is almost collinear with income (r = -0.98), and with GDP per capita controlled its coefficient is close to zero (-0.05, p = 0.92). The checks in our analysis plan do not make this robust: the coefficient is negative under every wage basis but significant only with ILOSTAT wages (p = 0.02), and leaving out single countries flips its sign six times. In this sample the tokenizer tax adds nothing detectable to income as a predictor of adoption. Data, code and figures are released under open licenses.
```
